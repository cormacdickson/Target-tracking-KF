The attention score is measured against your own calibrated ceiling: whatever tracking quality you produce during calibration becomes the zero line, so limited skill is absorbed into the baseline and the score reads deviation from it — attention, not ability.

The solid cyan line is a slow Kalman filter's estimate of that score; the dashed grey line is a simple moving average of the same readings. The filter adds what the average cannot: it weighs each new reading against how confident it already is, shows that confidence as the shaded band, widens the band honestly when readings stop arriving, and carries a trend term so it can follow a genuine decline without chasing every blip.

Try it: look away for a few seconds, then track badly on purpose while still following the dot — and see which one the estimate punishes. To be fair, with velocity mismatch as the only signal the two are only partially distinguishable.
